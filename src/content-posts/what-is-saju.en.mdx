If you've heard the word "saju" from a K-drama or a friend who's into Korean culture, you might be picturing something mysterious. It's actually a fairly systematic idea: saju (사주, literally "four pillars") is a reading built from the exact year, month, day, and hour you were born.

## The four pillars

Each of the four pillars pairs a "heavenly stem" with an "earthly branch" — together they form a 60-value cycle, a bit like how your birthday falls on a different day of the week each year. Your day pillar is considered the core of the reading, and the other three pillars add texture around it. Unlike a Western zodiac sign, which is a single fixed value set by your birth month, saju combines four separate values — so it can feel like a much more personal fingerprint.

## What it's used for today

In Korea, checking your saju is a casual, everyday thing — closer to reading a horoscope column than a formal ritual. People check in around big decisions, or just for fun, the same way friends elsewhere compare star signs.

## How Saju Letter uses it

The four pillars are worked out by a deterministic calendar algorithm, the same kind of calculation used for centuries — so the same birth date always gets the same result.
