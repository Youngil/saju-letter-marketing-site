Korea's lunar new year, Seollal (설날), is one of the country's biggest holidays — and it's also a meaningful date on the saju calendar.

## Why the date moves every year

Seollal follows the lunar calendar, so its date on the solar (Gregorian) calendar shifts each year, usually landing between late January and mid-February. It marks the second new moon after the winter solstice.

## A holiday, not a mystery

Seollal is a family holiday, similar in spirit to how many cultures gather to mark a new year — sharing food, visiting family, and starting the year with a clean slate. There's nothing esoteric about it; it's simply a well-loved seasonal tradition.

## What it means for your saju

Interestingly, Seollal isn't quite the same as the start of a new year on the saju calendar — the saju year actually turns over at ipchun (입춘), the first day of spring on the solar calendar, not on Seollal itself.
