If you know your zodiac sign, you already understand the basic idea behind saju — just with more detail layered in.

## The starting point is similar

Western astrology assigns you one sign based on your birth month. Saju uses your full birth date and time — year, month, day, and hour — to build four paired values instead of one.

## One fixed value vs. a combination that shifts daily

Your zodiac sign is set once and never changes. Saju's four pillars are fixed at the moment you're born too — but each day also has its own pillar, and how that day's pillar meets your chart is what shifts from one day to the next. That's part of why Saju Letter is built around a new letter each morning instead of one static profile.

## More inputs, more nuance

Because saju factors in the day and hour, not just the month, two people born a week apart can end up with noticeably different charts even if they'd share the same zodiac sign. That's part of why a saju reading can feel more specific to your exact birth moment.

## Neither one predicts the future

Both systems work the same way here: they're a lens for reflecting on tendencies and timing, not a guarantee of what will happen. Saju Letter keeps that in mind — every reading is meant to be a gentle, encouraging note, not a definitive prediction.
